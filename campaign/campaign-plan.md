# Security Awareness Campaign Plan

## Campaign Name

Think Before You Click

## Goal

The goal of this campaign is to help team members recognize common cybersecurity risks and follow simple security habits in their daily work.

The campaign mainly focuses on phishing, password security, multi-factor authentication and social engineering.

## Target Audience

The campaign is designed for all team members, including people without a technical cybersecurity background.

## Duration

The campaign will run for one week.

## Campaign Topics

### Day 1 - Phishing Awareness

Team members will learn how to recognize suspicious emails, links and attachments.

Main message:

Think before you click.

### Day 2 - Password Security

The focus will be on using strong and unique passwords and avoiding password reuse.

Multi-factor authentication will also be introduced as an extra security layer.

### Day 3 - Social Engineering

Team members will learn how attackers may use urgency, trust or impersonation to convince someone to share information or perform an unsafe action.

### Day 4 - Security Checklist

A short checklist will remind team members to:

- Check the sender before trusting an email
- Inspect links before opening them
- Avoid sharing passwords
- Use MFA when available
- Report suspicious activity

### Day 5 - Security Quiz

At the end of the campaign, team members will complete a short quiz covering the topics from the week.

## Educational Materials

The campaign includes:

- Phishing awareness guide
- Password security guide
- Social engineering guide
- Security awareness email
- Short cybersecurity quiz

These materials are stored in the materials folder.

## Delivery Method

The awareness material can be shared through company email or an internal communication platform.

A different security topic can be shared each day so that the information stays short and easy to understand.

## Measuring the Campaign

The campaign can be evaluated using the final security quiz.

The quiz can help check whether team members understand the basic security practices covered during the campaign.

The campaign can also collect feedback about which topics were useful or unclear.

## Expected Outcome

After the campaign, team members should be more careful with suspicious messages, use better password practices and know when they should report a possible security issue.
