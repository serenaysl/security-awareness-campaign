# Social Engineering Awareness

Social engineering is when an attacker tries to manipulate a person instead of directly attacking a computer system.

The attacker may pretend to be a trusted person or create pressure so that the victim acts quickly without checking the request.

## Common Techniques

Attackers may use:

- Urgent requests
- Fake support messages
- Impersonation of managers or coworkers
- Requests for passwords or verification codes
- Fake delivery or invoice messages
- Fear or pressure to make someone act quickly

## Example

A message says:

"I am your manager. I need you to send me the login code immediately. This is urgent."

This should be treated as suspicious because login codes should not be shared, even if the request appears to come from someone you know.

## How to Respond

If a request feels unusual:

1. Stop and check the request.
2. Verify the person's identity using another trusted method.
3. Do not share passwords or MFA codes.
4. Do not send sensitive company information without verification.
5. Report suspicious requests.

## Important Reminder

Attackers often try to create urgency so that people do not have time to think.

Taking a few seconds to verify a request can prevent a security problem.

## Key Message

Stop, verify and then act.
